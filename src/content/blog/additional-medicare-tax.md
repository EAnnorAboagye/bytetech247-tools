Most Medicare tax is simple: 1.45% of every dollar of wages, no cap, matched by your employer. The Additional Medicare Tax is a separate, extra 0.9% that only applies above a certain income level — and it works differently in two important ways.

## The thresholds

| Filing status             | Threshold |
| ------------------------- | --------- |
| Single                    | $200,000  |
| Head of household         | $200,000  |
| Married filing separately | $125,000  |
| Married filing jointly    | $250,000  |

Wages above your threshold are taxed an extra 0.9%, on top of the standard 1.45% Medicare rate — so income above the threshold is effectively taxed at 2.35% for Medicare, not 1.45%.

## Two things that make it different from regular Medicare tax

**It's employee-only.** Regular Medicare tax is matched dollar-for-dollar by your employer. The Additional Medicare Tax isn't — only the employee pays it, with no employer match at all.

**The thresholds don't move.** The Social Security wage base gets adjusted for inflation most years. The Additional Medicare Tax thresholds don't — they've been fixed at these exact numbers since the tax was introduced in 2013, and stay there until Congress changes them by statute.

## A worked example

A single filer earning $220,000:

- Income below the $200,000 threshold: taxed at the standard 1.45% Medicare rate
- Income from $200,000 to $220,000 ($20,000): taxed at 1.45% + 0.9% = 2.35%
- Additional Medicare Tax portion alone: 0.9% × $20,000 = **$180**

That $180 comes straight off net pay, the same way every other line in [Gross Pay vs. Net Pay](/blog/gross-pay-vs-net-pay) does.

## One filing-status wrinkle worth knowing

Because the married filing jointly threshold ($250,000) applies to combined household income, two spouses each earning $150,000 ($300,000 combined) would cross the threshold and owe Additional Medicare Tax on $50,000 of it — even though neither spouse individually earns above $200,000. This is a common surprise for dual-income households at that income level.
