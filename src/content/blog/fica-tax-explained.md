FICA stands for the Federal Insurance Contributions Act — it's the payroll tax that funds Social Security and Medicare. It shows up as its own line on every pay stub, separate from federal income tax withholding.

## The two parts of FICA

**Social Security: 6.2%.** This applies to wages up to the Social Security wage base — $184,500 for 2026. Earn more than that in a year, and Social Security tax stops being withheld on the excess. This cap is adjusted for inflation most years, so it changes annually.

**Medicare: 1.45%.** Unlike Social Security, there's no wage cap here — every dollar of wages is subject to the 1.45% Medicare rate, no matter how high your income goes.

Together, that's 7.65% of most paychecks (up to the Social Security wage base), split between these two programs.

## Your employer pays it too

FICA is a matched tax: your employer pays the same 6.2% and 1.45% out of their own funds, on top of what's withheld from your paycheck. You only ever see your own half on your pay stub — the employer's matching portion doesn't reduce your paycheck, but it's real money the government collects on your behalf regardless.

## The Additional Medicare Tax

Above a certain income threshold, an extra 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax kicks in — and unlike the base 1.45%, this one is employee-only; employers don't match it. The threshold depends on filing status:

| Filing status             | Threshold |
| ------------------------- | --------- |
| Single                    | $200,000  |
| Head of household         | $200,000  |
| Married filing separately | $125,000  |
| Married filing jointly    | $250,000  |

These thresholds are fixed by statute and haven't been inflation-adjusted since they were introduced in 2013 — they stay the same every year until Congress changes them, unlike the Social Security wage base above. See the full breakdown in [Additional Medicare Tax: Who Actually Pays It](/blog/additional-medicare-tax).

## A quick example

A single filer earning $75,000 pays:

- Social Security: 6.2% × $75,000 = $4,650
- Medicare: 1.45% × $75,000 = $1,087.50
- Additional Medicare Tax: $0 (below the $200,000 threshold)
- **Total FICA: $5,737.50**
